Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 7, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Country Days Tracker app (the "Service") touches, and what they deliberately do not. Country Days Tracker is a travel day tracker that counts the days you spend in each country against residency and visa rules, and it works entirely on your device.

In short: Country Days Tracker has no accounts and no sign-in, shows no ads, and never reads your location. Your trips, trackers and notes stay on your device and are never sent anywhere. Purchases are handled by Apple and Google. The app does use three third-party services - for purchases, for anonymous usage statistics and crash reports, and for measuring which ads brought an install - described in section 5. None of them receives your trips, countries, notes or tracker names, and we do not sell anyone’s data.

1. What we do not collect

Country Days Tracker has no user accounts and no registration - there is nothing to sign in to. The app shows no advertising and never uploads what you enter - your trips, trackers, countries, dates and notes - to us or to anyone else. The only data that leaves the device is the technical and usage data in section 5. It does not use GPS, does not run in the background to log where you are, and does not ask for location access: every trip in the app is one you typed in yourself. We do not know who you are, where you travel or which rules you track, and none of the services in section 5 is given that information.

2. What stays on your device

Everything you enter in Country Days Tracker is stored locally on your phone or tablet:

  • your trips - the country, the entry and exit dates, and any note you add;
  • your rule trackers and their settings (countries, limit, window, counting);
  • app preferences such as the theme, the color and the language;
  • whether you have bought Country Days Tracker Pro, cached so that the app works offline.

All day counts and forecasts are computed on the device from that data. Deleting the app deletes it from the device (subject to the platform backups described below).

A travel history is personal information - it says where you were and when. That is exactly why it never leaves your device unless you send it yourself.

3. Device backups

So that your travel history survives a lost phone or a reinstall, the app relies on the backup your platform already gives you - your own Apple or Google account, never a server of ours:

  • iOS: the app’s data is included in your iCloud or computer backup of the device, under Apple’s terms; we never see it.
  • Android: the app’s data is included in Android Auto Backup to your Google account, under Google’s terms; we never see it.

You can turn these backups off in your device settings. You can also export your trips as a CSV file and keep it wherever you like (see section 6).

4. Purchases

Country Days Tracker Pro (Monthly, Yearly, Lifetime) is sold through the Apple App Store and Google Play. Apple or Google process the payment; we never receive your name, email address or payment card details. The store tells the app whether a purchase is active, and the app keeps that status on the device. To check and restore purchases across reinstalls, the app uses RevenueCat, which receives the store’s purchase receipt together with a random anonymous identifier it generates for the install - see section 5. To cancel an auto-renewing plan, use your App Store or Google Play account settings.

5. Network use and third-party services

Country Days Tracker works offline: your trips, trackers and day counts are computed and kept on the device, there is no server of ours and no remote configuration. The app does talk to the following services. Each acts as a processor for the purpose named, and none of them is sent your trips, countries, dates, notes, tracker names or location.

  • Purchases - RevenueCat. To verify and restore Country Days Tracker Pro, the app sends the store’s purchase receipt and an anonymous RevenueCat identifier to RevenueCat (privacy policy). It is not linked to your name or email, which we do not have.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports - Google Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics. The app records anonymous events - which screen is opened, which plan is selected on the paywall and whether a purchase finished, which preset rule type or setting is chosen - and, if the app crashes, a crash report with the technical stack, the device model, the operating system and app version, the app language and the anonymous RevenueCat identifier. We use them to find bugs and to see which parts of the app are used. They are collected without the advertising identifier, are not used for advertising and do not contain anything you typed (Firebase privacy). Our own development builds do not send them.
  • Measuring ads - Meta. So that we can tell whether an ad on Facebook or Instagram brought an install or a purchase, the app includes Meta’s SDK, which reports the install and purchases in the app to Meta. On iPhone it uses the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you tap Allow on the system tracking question; if you decline, Meta receives only Apple’s anonymous, aggregated install counts. On Android it can use the advertising ID, which you can reset or delete in the Android settings (Privacy → Ads). The app shows no ads (Meta’s privacy policy).
  • Apple and Google run the store’s purchase flow under their own terms.

Reminders the app posts - for example when a limit is approaching - are local notifications, scheduled on the device from your own trip log. No push service, push token or server is involved, and you can switch them off in the app or in your device settings.

6. CSV export and sharing

When you export your trips, the app writes a CSV file on your device - the country, dates, day count and note of every trip - and hands it to the system share sheet. Where it goes - your accountant, a lawyer, your own files - is entirely your choice, and the app neither sees nor records the destination. Treat that file as you would any document with your travel history in it.

7. Children

Country Days Tracker is not directed to children under 13 (or under 16 in the European Economic Area), and - having no accounts - does not knowingly process personal data of anyone. If you believe a child’s personal data has reached us through support email, contact us and we will delete it.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the EU/UK GDPR or the California CCPA), you have rights over personal data - access, correction, export, deletion. Your trips never leave your device, so exercising them is in your own hands: the app exports them as CSV, and deleting a trip or the app deletes it. The anonymous identifiers in section 5 are held by the services named there; for those, and for anything you have sent us by email, contact us (see Contact) and we will help. You can also reset the advertising identifier or withdraw tracking permission in your device settings at any time.

9. Changes to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time - for example if a future version of the app adds an online feature such as sync or changes a service listed in section 5. When we do, we will revise the "Last updated" date above and, where the change is material, say so in the app’s release notes before it takes effect.
